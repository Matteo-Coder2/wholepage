Measuring the web, one page at a time

Field Notes · 14 min read

A web page has no bottom. It unrolls as you pull, loads as you look, and rearranges itself the moment you stop paying attention. To keep a record of one — a receipt, a contract, a conversation, a design — you need something closer to a surveyor's habit than a camera's reflex.

The oldest trick is the simplest: scroll, photograph, scroll again, and stitch the strips together like a panorama. It works exactly as well as the care taken at each seam. A pinned navigation bar photographed twelve times becomes a fence. An image that hadn't loaded yet becomes a gray hole in the record.

The seam problem

Every display draws at its own density. A page scrolled by exactly one screen has moved by one screen of logical pixels — but the record is kept in device pixels, and the ratio between the two is rarely a whole number. Rounding in the wrong place leaves a one-pixel scar at every joint; measuring instead of assuming leaves nothing.

“The instrument that measures the page must never change the page.”

The same discipline applies to what the page does while being measured. Animations pause. Videos hold their frame. The pinned bar appears once, where it belongs, and the record shows the page as a reader would remember it — not as a machine happened to photograph it.

What honest records refuse to do

They refuse to guess. A page that keeps growing as it is scrolled is recorded to a stated depth, and the record says so. A page wider than the window is captured at its visible width, and the record says that too. Nothing is trimmed in silence.

ConditionHonest behavior
Endless feedCapture what is loaded; say how much
Very long pageOffer pages, slices, or scale — never cut
Pinned toolbarOnce, at the top
Unloaded imagesWait, then record

None of this is exotic engineering. It is bookkeeping — the patient kind, done the same way every time, checked against a ruler that never flatters. A record you can trust is mostly the accumulation of small refusals to approximate.

So the next time a page matters — the order confirmation, the apartment listing, the paragraph that will be edited by morning — take its measure properly. Top to bottom, seam by seam, nothing missing and nothing twice.
